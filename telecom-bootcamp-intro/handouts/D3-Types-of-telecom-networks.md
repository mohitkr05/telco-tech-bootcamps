# Types of Telecommunication systems

The types of Telecommunication systems can be categorized based on different criterias like:

 - Transmission medium
 - Network Topology
 - Signal Type
 - Service Type
 - Ownership and operation

## Transmission medium

### Wired medium

Guided transmission medium is a physical medium that confines the electromagnetic waves within a specific path. This type of medium is typically used for wired networks

 - Twisted pair copper wire
 - Coaxial cable
 - Optical Fibre
    
### Wireless medium

Unguided transmission medium does not confine the electromagnetic waves within a specific path. Instead, the waves are radiated into the surrounding environment. This type of medium is typically used for wireless networks.


 - Cellular networks
 - Wireless LANs
 - Wireless PANs
 - Microwave communications
 - Satellite communication

## Network Topology

Network topology is the physical and logical arrangement of nodes and connections in a network. Nodes are devices such as computers, switches, and routers. 

### PAN
A personal area network (PAN) is a network of interconnected devices that are located within a user's immediate area. PANs typically have a range of 10 meters or less, and they can be used to connect devices such as computers, smartphones, tablets, wearables, and printers.


### LAN
A local area network (LAN) is a network of connected devices that are typically located in the same physical location, such as a home, office, or school. LANs are typically used to share resources such as files, printers, and internet access.

### WAN
A wide area network (WAN) is a network of connected devices that are located over a large geographical area, such as a city, country, or even the world. WANs are typically used to connect LANs together and to provide access to resources such as the internet.

### Broadcast

A broadcast network is a network in which all devices on the network receive all transmissions. This is in contrast to a switched network, in which transmissions are only sent to the intended destination device.


## Signal Type

### Analog

Analog signals are continuous signals that can have any value within a certain range. They are typically represented by waveforms, such as sine waves, square waves, and triangle waves. Analog signals are used in a variety of applications, such as audio, video, and temperature sensors.

Examples : 
- Audio signals from a microphone
- Video signals from a camera
- Temperature readings from a thermometer

### Digital

Digital signals are discrete signals that can only have a finite number of values. They are typically represented by binary digits, or bits, which can be either 0 or 1. Digital signals are used in a variety of applications, such as computers, digital cameras, and digital music players.

Examples: 
- Data stored on a computer hard drive
- Images on a digital display
- Sound waves from a digital music player

## Service Type

### Voice services
- Voice services allow users to communicate with each other using their voice. This includes traditional landline telephone service, as well as mobile phone service and VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol) services.

### Data services

Data services allow users to transmit data over a telecommunications network. This includes internet access, as well as other services such as email, file transfer, and remote access.

### Video services

Video services allow users to transmit and receive video over a telecommunications network. This includes television broadcasting, video conferencing, and video streaming services.

### Cloud-based telecom networks

Cloud-based telecoms services are delivered over the internet, rather than through traditional telecommunications networks. This includes services such as hosted VoIP, cloud-based PBX systems, and cloud-based video conferencing

- Public networks - Carrier networks
